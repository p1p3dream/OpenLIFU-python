**Column definitions:**

- **Rank**: Ordinal rank by increasing fullwave-to-water attenuation.
- **Subject**: De-identified subject identifier. Prefix indicates acquisition site: GU (Georgetown University), NC (University of North Carolina), NYU (New York University), subj (pilot cohort).
- **Atten (dB)**: Transcranial attenuation measured as 20 log10(p_water / p_corr), where p_water is the free-water focal pressure and p_corr is the phase-corrected through-skull focal pressure from the fullwave simulation.
- **n_clear**: Number of array elements (out of 64) whose central ray does not intersect the skull segmentation mask. Elements with skull intersection require phase and amplitude correction.
- **Bone %**: Cortical bone percentage at the nearest-element skull intersection point, derived from the CT-based three-layer skull model (outer table, diploe, inner table).
- **Skull Path (mm)**: Minimum skull thickness traversed by any element's acoustic ray. A value of 0.00 indicates the nearest element has a clear path (no skull intersection at the element closest to the target).
- **Focal Offset (mm)**: Euclidean distance between the peak-pressure location in the corrected simulation and the intended geometric focal point.
- **Tx Ratio**: Transmit efficiency, defined as p_corr / p_water. Values below 1.0 indicate attenuation by the skull; lower values correspond to greater transcranial loss.
- **Corr/Geom**: Ratio of corrected focal pressure to geometric-only (uncorrected) focal pressure, quantifying the benefit of the continuous-wave aberration correction relative to a naive geometric focusing approach.
- **Orientation (theta, phi)**: Transducer placement angles in degrees. Theta is the polar angle from the superior axis; phi is the azimuthal angle measured from the anterior direction. These define the optimized array pose relative to the subject's skull.

## Appendix B: Pipeline Technical Specifications

### B.1 Acoustic Simulation Parameters

| Parameter | Value | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Simulation engine | Fullwave 2.0 (finite-difference time-domain) | Nonlinear acoustic propagation |
| Spatial discretization | 0.2 mm isotropic | Approximately 15 points per wavelength at 500 kHz in water |
| Temporal discretization | 20 ns | CFL condition satisfied for all tissue types |
| Grid dimensions | Subject-dependent, typically 400 x 400 x 300 voxels | Encompasses full array aperture and focal region |
| Boundary conditions | Perfectly matched layers (PML), 20 voxels thick | Absorbing boundaries on all six faces |
| Source model | 64-element phased array, per-element point sources | Phase and amplitude independently controlled |
| Transmit frequency | 500 kHz | Fundamental frequency, continuous-wave excitation |
| Number of cycles | 40 cycles steady-state | Sufficient for CW convergence at focal depth |
| Pressure extraction | Peak positive pressure at steady state | Spatial peak over 3D focal volume |

### B.2 Tissue Acoustic Properties

| Tissue | Speed of Sound (m/s) | Density (kg/m^3) | Attenuation (dB/cm/MHz) | Source |
|--------|--------------------:|------------------:|------------------------:|--------|
| Water (degassed, 37C) | 1524 | 994 | 0.0022 | Calibrated measurement |
| Soft tissue (scalp) | 1540 | 1050 | 0.6 | Duck (1990) |
| Cortical bone | 2850 | 1900 | 4.0 | Pichardo et al. (2011) |
| Trabecular bone (diploe) | 2100 | 1400 | 8.0 | Pichardo et al. (2011) |
| Brain parenchyma | 1546 | 1046 | 0.6 | IT'IS Foundation v4.0 |

### B.3 Skull Model Construction

| Step | Method | Details |
|------|--------|---------|
| CT acquisition | Clinical diagnostic CT | 0.5 mm in-plane, 0.5-1.0 mm slice thickness |
| Skull segmentation | Three-layer model | Outer cortical table, diploe, inner cortical table |
| Bone mapping | CT Hounsfield units to acoustic properties | Linear mapping within cortical/trabecular ranges |
| Registration | CT-to-MRI rigid registration | Mutual information, 6 DOF |
| Coordinate transform | MRI native space to array coordinates | Via fiducial-based or surface-based registration |

### B.4 Phase Correction Method

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Correction type | Continuous-wave (CW) time-reversal | 
| Virtual source | Point source at geometric focus |
| Propagation model | Fullwave FDTD through heterogeneous skull |
| Phase extraction | Per-element phase at array surface |
| Amplitude correction | Not applied (phase-only correction) |
| Correction metric | Maximizes focal pressure at target |

## Appendix C: Figure Captions

### Figure 1: Distribution of Transcranial Attenuation Across 64 Subjects

Histogram of fullwave-to-water attenuation (dB) for all 64 subjects in the validation cohort. Each bar represents a 2 dB bin. The distribution spans 5.43 to 20.77 dB with a mean of 11.76 dB (SD 3.78) and median of 11.83 dB. The interquartile range [8.98, 13.71] dB captures the central bulk of the distribution. Color coding indicates acquisition site (Georgetown, blue; UNC, orange; NYU, green; Pilot, gray). The vertical dashed line marks the cohort median. Subjects with attenuation below approximately 9 dB represent favorable acoustic windows where more than 35% of transmitted pressure reaches the focus, while subjects exceeding 17 dB represent challenging cases with less than 15% pressure transmission. The roughly symmetric distribution with a slight right tail is consistent with the known variability of skull acoustic properties across adults.

### Figure 2: Clear Element Count vs. Transcranial Attenuation

Scatter plot of the number of unobstructed array elements (n_clear, out of 64) versus fullwave-to-water attenuation (dB) for 61 subjects with complete placement data. Each point represents one subject, colored by acquisition site. The ordinary least-squares regression line (solid) and 95% confidence band (shaded) are overlaid. A weak negative correlation is expected, as subjects with more clear elements tend to have less skull material in the beam path, though attenuation depends on additional factors including bone density, cortical thickness, and diploe fraction. Subjects in the upper-left quadrant (high n_clear, low attenuation) represent the most favorable treatment geometries. The wide scatter at intermediate n_clear values (15-35) reflects the dominant influence of bone composition over element count alone. Three subjects without placement data (NC004, GU010, GU002) are excluded.

### Figure 3: Attenuation Stratified by Clear Element Count

Box plot of transcranial attenuation grouped by binned clear element count. Subjects are divided into four bins based on n_clear: very few (less than 10), few (10-19), moderate (20-34), and many (35+). Each box shows the median (center line), interquartile range (box edges), and 1.5x IQR whiskers, with individual data points overlaid as jittered dots. The "many" bin is expected to show a lower median attenuation, though substantial overlap between groups demonstrates that element clearance alone is an incomplete predictor of transcranial attenuation. This figure complements Figure 2 by highlighting the distributional overlap rather than the trend line, reinforcing that skull composition at the intersection points matters as much as the number of obstructed paths.

### Figure 4: Continuous-Wave Correction Benefit Across Attenuation Levels

Scatter plot of the Corr/Geom ratio (corrected focal pressure divided by geometric-only focal pressure) as a function of transcranial attenuation (dB) for all 64 subjects. The Corr/Geom ratio quantifies the benefit of CW phase correction relative to naive geometric focusing. Values below 1.0 indicate that the corrected simulation achieves lower focal pressure than the geometric prediction, which is expected because the geometric model does not account for skull-induced losses. Values closer to 1.0 would indicate that CW correction fully recovers the geometric prediction. The cohort mean Corr/Geom ratio is 0.598 (SD 0.105), indicating that on average, the CW-corrected focal pressure reaches approximately 60% of the geometric prediction. A LOWESS smoothing curve (solid line) reveals the trend across attenuation levels. At low attenuation (less than 8 dB), the ratio ranges from 0.57 to 0.73, while at high attenuation (greater than 17 dB), it drops to 0.27-0.49, consistent with progressively greater aberration and absorption that phase-only correction cannot fully compensate.

### Figure 5: Distribution of Focal Offset

(A) Histogram of Euclidean focal offset (mm) between the corrected-simulation pressure peak and the geometric focal point for all 64 subjects. The distribution is tightly concentrated, with a mean of 0.232 mm (SD 0.070 mm), a median of 0.244 mm, and all subjects falling below 0.35 mm. Fifty-seven of 64 subjects (89%) achieved offsets below 0.30 mm, and 18 subjects (28%) fell below 0.20 mm. The vertical dashed line marks the cohort mean. (B) Focal offset plotted against transcranial attenuation, showing no systematic dependence of spatial accuracy on attenuation severity. The CW correction maintains sub-millimeter targeting accuracy across the full attenuation range, from the most favorable (5.43 dB) to the most challenging (20.77 dB) acoustic windows. The horizontal dashed line at 1.0 mm marks a commonly cited clinical targeting tolerance for neuromodulation applications; all subjects fall well below this threshold.

### Figure 6: Array Geometry and Placement Optimization

(A) Top-down view of the 64-element sparse random phased array, showing element positions distributed in a pseudo-random Fermat spiral pattern across the 110 mm aperture. Element diameter is 6.6 mm. The geometric focus lies at 72 mm depth (F-number 0.65). Twelve distinct placement orientations were used across the 64-subject cohort, reflecting the finite set of target locations and the constrained optimization over skull geometry.
